01Startup money 101

To understand who Ksenia is, you first need to know how startups get money.

03Limina, from zero

The problem, in a story

A bank wants to use AI to read thousands of customer support chats and find what customers are unhappy about. But those chats are full of names, phone numbers, account numbers and addresses. If that private information goes into an AI tool, the bank could break privacy laws, leak data, or lose customer trust.

So the bank is stuck: the data is valuable, but it's too risky to use.

The solution

Limina finds the private information and hides it or swaps it for safe fake values, before the data goes into AI. The meaning of the chat stays, but the private details are gone. Now the bank can use the data safely.

Before: "Hi, I'm Sarah Chen, my card ending 4421 was charged twice."
After (redacted): "Hi, I'm [NAME_1], my card ending [CARD_1] was charged twice."
After (contextual replacement): "Hi, I'm Maria Lopez, my card ending 8807 was charged twice." (realistic, but fake)

Key facts to remember

  • Was called Private AI. It grew into Limina.
  • Started in 2019 by privacy and machine-learning experts from the University of Toronto.
  • Based in Toronto (Adelaide St W).
  • Their line: "Every prompt is a data transaction, and Limina governs every one."
  • Works in 52 languages.
  • Runs inside the customer's own systems, so data never leaves.
  • Helps with laws like HIPAA, GDPR, CPRA, PCI-DSS.
  • Customers: Zurich Insurance, MUFG Bank, RBC, Intact Insurance, Boehringer Ingelheim, Deloitte.

Latest news

September 14, 2026: Limina launched Contextual Replacement in release 4.5. It replaces private details with realistic fake ones that fit the sentence, so the data stays useful for AI and analytics.

Why now? (the "perfect market timing")

Every company is rushing to use AI. Every AI project needs data. Most good data has private information inside. So every company building AI needs something like Limina. That's why the job post says "Every organization building AI is grappling with how to govern sensitive data inside it."

07The funnel

The funnel is the journey from "never heard of us" to "paying customer". Lots of people at the top, fewer at each step. Demand gen fills the top and helps people move down.
Awareness (TOFU) They learn Limina exists: ads, posts, webinars
Interest They read, download, register: now a lead
Consideration (MOFU) They engage more: MQL
Sales conversation MDR books a call: SQL / meeting
Opportunity (BOFU) A real deal in progress: pipeline
Customer Closed won

TOFU, MOFU, BOFU

  • TOFU (top of funnel): get noticed. Ads, LinkedIn posts, blogs.
  • MOFU (middle): teach and build trust. Webinars, guides, case studies.
  • BOFU (bottom): help them decide. Demos, ROI, customer proof.

Demand gen vs lead gen

Lead gen collects contact details (forms, downloads). Demand gen is bigger: it makes the right people want the product, then captures them. A great webinar is demand gen. The registration form is lead gen.

Sourced vs influenced pipeline

  • Sourced: the deal started because of marketing (first came in from a webinar).
  • Influenced: marketing touched the deal along the way (an existing prospect attended a webinar).

Lead scoring

Points that decide when a lead becomes an MQL. Two kinds:

  • Fit (who they are): right title, industry, company size. Example: Privacy Director at a bank = +30.
  • Engagement (what they do): attended a webinar +20, visited the pricing page +15, opened an email +2.

At 60 points, they become an MQL and go to the MDR.

The people

  • MDR / SDR / BDR: the person who contacts leads and books meetings. Limina has an MDR you'll work closely with.
  • AE (Account Executive): runs the deal and closes it.
  • CS (Customer Success): keeps customers happy after they buy. Helps with case studies.

Attribution

Deciding which marketing gets credit for a deal.

  • First touch: credit to the first thing they did (the LinkedIn ad).
  • Last touch: credit to the last thing before converting (the demo request email).
  • Multi-touch: credit shared across everything. Fairer, but more complex.
  • Self-reported: the form asks "How did you hear about us?" Simple and often very useful.

10Tools: CRM, HubSpot, UTMs

CRM

Customer Relationship Management: the database of every contact, company and deal. Examples: HubSpot, Salesforce.

Marketing automation (MAP)

Software that sends emails, runs forms and landing pages, scores leads and moves them through stages automatically. HubSpot does both CRM and automation. The job post names HubSpot.

HubSpot words to know

  • Contact, company, deal: the three main records.
  • Properties: fields on a record (job title, industry, lead source).
  • Lists: active lists update by themselves; static lists don't change.
  • Workflows: automations. "If someone registers, send confirmation, add to list, notify MDR."
  • Lifecycle stage: subscriber, lead, MQL, SQL, opportunity, customer.
  • Forms and landing pages: where people sign up.
  • Sequences: one-to-one sales email series (used by the MDR).

UTMs (tracking links)

Little tags added to a link so you know where a visitor came from.
getlimina.ai/webinar?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=paid-social&utm_campaign=2026-11-ai-governance-webinar&utm_content=ad-a
  • source: where (linkedin, newsletter)
  • medium: what kind (paid-social, email)
  • campaign: which campaign
  • content: which ad or link version

The form captures these into hidden fields, so HubSpot knows exactly which ad brought each lead.

Other tools you may meet

LinkedIn Campaign Manager (ads), Google Analytics (website traffic), ZoomInfo / Apollo (contact data), Zoom / ON24 / Goldcast (webinars), G2 (reviews), 6sense / Bombora (intent data), Canva / Figma (design).

11ABM, deep dive

ABM (Account-Based Marketing) flips the funnel. Instead of fishing with a big net for anyone, you pick a short list of companies you really want, then market to the people at those companies, in a personal way.

Big net vs spear

Normal demand gen

Run an ad for all privacy leaders. See who signs up. Hope the right companies come.

ABM

Pick 25 banks and insurers. Find the 3 to 5 key people at each. Send them tailored content, ads, emails and even a gift. Sales and marketing work together on every account.

The 3 tiers

TierHow many accountsHow personal
1:1 (strategic)5 to 10Fully custom. Custom page, gift, exec outreach. (Your box for Tyler was a 1:1 play!)
1:few (cluster)10 to 50, groupedCustom per group, like "Canadian banks" or "US health insurers".
1:many (programmatic)HundredsLight personal touches, mostly targeted ads.

ABM, step by step

  1. Define the ICP (Ideal Customer Profile): industry, size, region, signals. Example: banks and insurers with 1,000+ employees in North America, building AI.
  2. Pick the accounts with sales. Use fit plus signals (hiring AI roles, a recent data breach in the news, new privacy rules).
  3. Map the buying committee: privacy, security, data, AI and legal leaders at each account.
  4. Research and message: what does each account care about? Write tailored messages.
  5. Run the plays: LinkedIn ads targeted to only those companies, personal emails from the MDR, invites to an exclusive roundtable, a custom one-pager, a gift.
  6. Coordinate with the MDR: marketing warms them up; the MDR follows up at the right moment.
  7. Measure: are the right accounts engaging, and is pipeline coming from them?

ABM metrics

  • Account coverage: do we have contacts for the key people at each account?
  • Account engagement: how many target accounts visited, clicked, attended?
  • Meetings booked with target accounts.
  • Pipeline and win rate from target accounts.

Intent data

Signals that a company is researching a topic right now (for example, many people at a bank reading about "PII redaction"). Tools like 6sense or Bombora sell this. It tells you who to focus on this month.

"For the ABM pilot, I'd start small: 25 accounts with the MDR, mapped to 3 to 5 people each, one tailored offer per cluster, and weekly check-ins on engagement and meetings. Prove it on a few accounts, then scale."

13Email programs

  • Newsletter: regular update to the whole database.
  • Promotional email: one campaign, like a webinar invite.
  • Nurture: a series that teaches over weeks, slowly building trust.
  • Segmentation: different emails for different groups (banks vs health).
  • A/B test: send two versions to small groups, then send the winner to the rest.
  • Deliverability: making sure emails reach the inbox, not spam. Keep lists clean and don't buy random lists.
  • CASL: Canada's anti-spam law. You need permission (consent) to email people, and an easy unsubscribe.

A good B2B email

  1. A short, clear subject line
  2. One idea
  3. Why it matters to them
  4. One call to action (one button)
  5. Easy to read on a phone

14LinkedIn ads and paid media

LinkedIn is the best place to reach business buyers by their job title, company and industry. It costs more than other networks, so targeting and creative must be sharp.

Targeting

  • Job titles or functions: Privacy, Security, Data, Legal.
  • Seniority: Director, VP, C-level.
  • Company lists: upload target accounts (great for ABM).
  • Industry and company size.
  • Retargeting: people who visited the website or watched a video.
  • Matched audiences: upload a contact list.

Ad formats

Single image, carousel, video, document ads (a PDF people can read in the feed), conversation ads (inbox messages), thought leader ads (boosting an exec's post).

Lead Gen Forms

The form opens inside LinkedIn, pre-filled. Easier for people, so more leads, but sometimes lower quality. Sync them to HubSpot.

Your part in paid

  • Audience building: the targeting above.
  • Ad copy: headline, intro text, call to action.
  • Performance tracking: CTR, CPC, CPL, MQLs, cost per meeting. Pause what's weak, scale what works.
  • Content syndication: paying a partner to share your guide with their audience, and you get the leads.

16Events

The job says "so prospects know we'll be there and book time with us before we arrive." That means: don't just show up at a conference. Fill the calendar before it starts.
  • Before: get the attendee list if the event shares one, find target accounts, email and LinkedIn "we'll be at booth 12, want to meet?", MDR books meetings, post on social.
  • During: post photos, scan badges, note conversations.
  • After: follow up within 48 hours, add leads to HubSpot, report meetings and pipeline.

Examples of events in this space: privacy and security conferences like IAPP gatherings, RSA Conference, and industry events for banking, insurance and health data leaders.

17Case studies

A case study is a customer's story: their problem, how Limina helped, and the result. It's the strongest proof for new buyers. Your job is the logistics, from first ask to final sign-off.
  1. Source: ask Customer Success and Sales who's happy and getting results.
  2. Ask: make it easy and show what they get (exposure, a backlink, a nice story).
  3. Schedule: book a 30-minute interview, send questions ahead.
  4. Interview and write (or brief the writer).
  5. Approvals: big companies need legal and PR sign-off. This often takes the longest. Track it.
  6. Publish and use: web page, PDF, sales deck slide, LinkedIn post, email.
Regulated companies (banks, insurers) often can't be named. Plan for anonymous versions: "A top-10 North American bank".

18Sales enablement (rep assets)

"Rep" means a sales rep. Rep assets are materials salespeople use in follow-ups:

  • One-pagers per industry (banking, health, insurance)
  • Follow-up email templates
  • Case study summaries
  • Battlecards: how Limina compares with other options
  • Objection handling: "We built our own redaction" → answer
  • Slides and short videos

"Adapting messaging for new industries" means taking the core message and rewriting it with that industry's words, risks and laws.

19Lists and data

  • Target list: the right people at the right companies.
  • Import: bringing contacts into HubSpot from events, partners or tools.
  • Cleaning: removing duplicates, fixing job titles, filling missing industry, removing bounced emails.
  • Segmentation: tagging people so you can target them (persona, industry, region, stage).
  • Enrichment: adding missing details with a tool like ZoomInfo or Apollo.
  • Consent: keep track of who agreed to receive emails (CASL in Canada, GDPR in Europe).
"I see list cleanup, form testing and UTM tracking as part of the craft. Bad data breaks every campaign after it."

20Reporting

The job post: "what's driving new leads, MQLs, and pipeline, and what isn't. Recommend where to shift effort."

A simple weekly report

  1. Numbers: leads, MQLs, meetings, pipeline. This week vs last week vs target.
  2. By channel: webinar, email, LinkedIn, events, partners.
  3. What worked, what didn't, and why.
  4. Recommendation: "Move $2K from display to LinkedIn document ads; CPL is 40% lower there."
"Numbers first, then the story: what moved, why, and what we'll do next."
